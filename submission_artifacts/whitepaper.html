Abstract

Urban freshwater streams represent critical ecological buffer zones that directly modulate human environmental health. The European Union Horizon Europe OneAquaHealth project monitors urban stream corridors across five European pilot cities (Coimbra, Toulouse, Benevento, Ghent, Oslo) through participatory citizen science. However, conventional citizen science pipelines suffer from a structural quality bottleneck: 100% of incoming submissions require manual expert verification, leading to 60-80% rejection rates, severe researcher burnout, volunteer feedback latency exceeding weeks, and over 90% volunteer churn within 30 days. Most critically, validated ecological data remains trapped in isolated databases, severed from public health early warning networks.

StreamSense introduces an autonomous, multi-agent AI triage platform that bridges raw citizen observation and standardized healthcare informatics. Utilizing a 7-agent pipeline combining multimodal vision (Gemini 3 Flash series: gemini-3.8-flash / 3.7 / 3.5-flash-lite), high-throughput NLP (Groq Qwen 3.8 27B / GPT-OSS cascade), geospatial/biodiversity verification (GBIF, OpenStreetMap), and weighted Bayesian quality scoring (Gemini 3 Flash), StreamSense completes end-to-end triage in 3 to 5 seconds. High-confidence observations (score ≥ 70) are auto-validated into HL7 FHIR R4 health records conforming to the observation-indicators-oah profile. Ambiguous submissions (score < 70) are routed to an expert queue accompanied by an automated AI Expert Brief, reducing researcher review time from 10 minutes to 30 seconds. Every citizen receives an instant, plain-language One Health Impact Receipt.

1. The Problem: The 5-Stage Citizen Science Quality Cascade

Across urban European catchments, citizen science is the only financially viable mechanism to achieve high-density spatial and temporal environmental surveillance. Yet, without an intelligent triage intermediary, citizen monitoring fails across five cascading stages:

  • 1. Unstructured Submission: Citizen submits a smartphone photo with vague descriptions (e.g., "water looks cloudy, saw some bugs"). Lack of immediate taxonomic and environmental structuring degrades scientific utility.
  • 2. Manual Queue Congestion: Every observation queues for manual inspection by academic freshwater ecologists, creating weeks of backlog.
  • 3. High Rejection & Wasted Labor: Researchers discard 60-80% of submissions due to photographic blur, invalid GPS, or lack of diagnostic bioindicator value.
  • 4. Rapid Volunteer Churn: Lack of reciprocal feedback causes >90% of citizen volunteers to abandon participation within 30 days.
  • 5. Severed One Health Integration: Validated bioindicator data remains in siloed CSVs, unable to reach public health epidemiological surveillance systems prior to clinical outbreak.
The StreamSense Operational Hypothesis
Citizens do not inherently produce invalid data; rather, current systems lack an automated, real-time quality triage filter. By applying multi-agent triage, approximately 80% of submissions can be auto-validated and standardized instantly, reserving expert human attention strictly for the ~20% of complex, boundary cases.

Agent Functional Specifications

# Agent Name Engine / Model Latency Scientific Objective & Responsibilities
1 Vision Analyzer Gemini 3 Flash (3.8 / 3.7 / 3.5-lite) ~2.0s Identifies benthic macroinvertebrates from photos against 15 target taxa; assigns BMWP scores and flags disease vector taxa (Culicidae, Simuliidae).
2 Description Interpreter Groq (qwen/qwen3.8-27b) <500ms Parses free-text volunteer notes into 14 standardized parameters: water clarity, color, flow velocity, odor, algae cover, debris, and bank substrate.
3 Metadata Validator Groq (qwen/qwen3.8-27b) + APIs <800ms Verifies coordinates against OSM water boundaries (≤50m), confirms temporal integrity, fetches weather, and cross-references GBIF species distribution within 200km.
4 Quality Scorer Gemini 3 Flash (3.8 / 3.7) ~2.0s Computes weighted composite score (0-100); enforces strict guardrails; outputs transparent explainability breakdown and routing verdict.
5 FHIR Translator Gemini 3 Flash (3.8 / 3.7) ~2.0s Converts validated data into HL7 FHIR R4 Observation resource conforming to observation-indicators-oah; includes 2-stage self-healing schema retry loop.
6 Impact Generator Groq (qwen/qwen3.8-27b) <500ms Translates technical findings into warm, encouraging 4-part Impact Receipts connecting citizen findings directly to neighborhood One Health outcomes.
7 Expert Brief Generator Gemini 3 Flash (3.8 / 3.7) ~2.0s Pre-annotates low-confidence submissions with typed concerns (severity, check recommendations, review time estimate) to streamline expert review.

4. Mathematical Quality Scoring & Dual-Routing Algorithm

The central decision engine (Agent 4) evaluates observation plausibility using a calibrated, transparent weighted score: 

Total Score = 0.40 * S_vision + 0.35 * S_metadata + 0.25 * S_description
  • Vision Score ($S_{vision}$, 0-40 pts): Calibrated on taxonomic identification certainty. Top confidence ≥ 0.80 yields 40 pts; ≥ 0.50 yields 25 pts; ≥ 0.30 yields 15 pts; < 0.30 or failure yields 5 pts.
  • Metadata Score ($S_{metadata}$, 0-35 pts): Evaluates physical and biological plausibility. Zero detected anomalies yields 35 pts; 1 minor anomaly yields 25 pts; ≥2 minor or 1 major anomaly (e.g. coordinates in open ocean or 2km from nearest stream) yields 10 pts; invalid metadata yields 0 pts.
  • Description Score ($S_{description}$, 0-25 pts): Evaluates observational richness. High extraction confidence with ≥5 parameters yields 25 pts; ≥3 parameters yields 18 pts; minimal description yields 10 pts; missing text yields 5 pts.
Autonomous Guardrails & Deterministic Fallbacks
To guarantee research integrity: (1) If the vision model fails to identify an organism, total score is capped at 50 (mandatory human review). (2) If GPS coordinates fail geographic validity checks, score is capped at 30. (3) If external AI providers experience outages, an embedded deterministic rule engine calculates the score, ensuring the pipeline never deadlocks.

Operationalizing the One Health Nexus

When urban freshwater streams experience organic enrichment and thermal stress, sensitive predatory taxa (Plecoptera, Ephemeroptera) vanish. Flow stagnation and nutrient concentration create ideal breeding reservoirs for Culicidae (Culex pipiens, vector of West Nile Virus; Aedes albopictus, vector of Dengue and Chikungunya). StreamSense identifies vector larvae in field photos, flagging them with disease-vector-detected: true. This triggers an automated epidemiological alert, bridging environmental monitoring directly to municipal vector control programs (such as the consortium's DipteraCAST predictive forecasting platform).

7. Security Architecture, Privacy, and FAIR Principles

StreamSense implements robust defense-in-depth security engineered for vulnerable environmental and citizen data:

  • Transport & Session Security: Strict end-to-end TLS 1.3 encryption. JWT authentication tokens are maintained exclusively inside httpOnly, SameSite=Lax, encrypted cookies, mitigating Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) exfiltration risks.
  • Zero-Trust Data Protection: All database operations utilize SQLAlchemy parameterized statements to eliminate SQL injection. Input payloads are strictly validated against Pydantic v2 schemas before reaching business logic.
  • Citizen Privacy Preservation: In compliance with GDPR and research ethics guidelines, volunteer identifying details (names, email addresses, personal phone EXIF metadata) are scrubbed prior to FHIR serialization. In public FHIR records, the observation performer is coded anonymously as "Citizen Scientist (StreamSense Volunteer)".
  • FAIR Data Principles Compliance:
    • Findable: Every record is assigned an immutable UUID and indexed with standardized geospatial and taxonomic descriptors.
    • Accessible: Observations are queryable via open REST endpoints with standard JSON/GeoJSON serializations.
    • Interoperable: Native alignment with HL7 FHIR R4, LOINC, and BMWP standards eliminates vendor lock-in.
    • Reusable: Full cryptographic and analytical provenance is preserved: raw citizen descriptions, per-agent JSON outputs, quality breakdown vectors, and expert reviewer audit logs.

9. Conclusion and Future Roadmap

StreamSense proves that artificial intelligence, when architected with multi-agent specialization and grounded in domain standards, transforms citizen science from an unmanageable burden into an authoritative environmental surveillance network. By automating 80% of routine triage and standardizing ecological data into HL7 FHIR R4 health records, StreamSense bridges the historic chasm between freshwater ecology and public health.

The next developmental phases will incorporate direct live integration with the official OneAquaHealth Citizen Science mobile application, automated bidirectional data exchange with ENORA Innovation's DipteraCAST disease forecasting model, and local language localization across Portuguese, French, Italian, Dutch, and Norwegian.
